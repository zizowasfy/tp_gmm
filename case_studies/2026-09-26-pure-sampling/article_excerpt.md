We compared Cartesian-IK and joint-projected GMM sampling with unrestricted joint-space OMPL sampling using RRTConnect, a 3 s planning budget and a covariance cutoff of 2.0. Uniform fallback was disabled both in the custom samplers and in MoveIt’s sampler wrapper. An initial paired study comprised 60 randomly generated endpoint-feasible environments and ten repeats per method (1,800 plans). Cartesian-IK and projection achieved audited success rates of 94.83% and 94.67%, respectively; paired environment-level inference found no superiority in success or failure-penalized runtime. An independent confirmation study used 60 new environments and five repeats per method (900 plans). Among 281 matched successful pairs in 57 environments, projection reduced total joint travel by 0.801 rad (95% stratified cluster-bootstrap CI 0.147–1.481; prespecified one-sided permutation test, Holm-adjusted p=0.043). The corresponding EE path-length reduction was not confirmed (p=0.122). We therefore selected projection on the basis of lower joint travel, without claiming improved reliability or planning speed. Unrestricted OMPL succeeded on all measured requests but produced longer unsimplified paths. Its larger feasible region makes this a comparison of complete planning configurations; the result does not establish performance for the subsequent EV disassembly task.
