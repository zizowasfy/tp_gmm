We evaluated clearance sensitivity by replaying 120 previously generated environments with identical joint endpoints, obstacle geometry, prior TP-GMM, RL policy and planner/sampler settings. The original normalized clearance levels 0.2 and 0.8 were mapped to 0.5 and 0.7, respectively, retaining 2,700 plans across Cartesian–IK, joint projection and unrestricted OMPL sampling. Only the 0.8→0.7 subset tests a clearance reduction. Both GMM samplers used cutoff 2.0 with uniform fallback disabled; projected sampling used no online IK rescue. Live collision scenes were verified against the frozen inputs, and returned paths underwent a common dense audit. An initial replay attempt with a reproduced scene-restoration defect was excluded in full and both cohorts were restarted after correcting and verifying restoration.

For 0.8→0.7, Cartesian–IK failures decreased from 41/450 to 20/450 and projected failures from 45/450 to 22/450. Shared start/corridor incompatibilities decreased from 40 to 20 requests per method, largely through recovery of two environments. Environment-weighted success improvements were 3.67 percentage points (95% cluster interval 0.00–8.67; Holm-adjusted p=0.500) and 4.17 points (0.17–9.17; p=0.375), respectively. Thus, the observed improvement was not statistically decisive at the environment level. Raising clearance from 0.2 to 0.5 introduced additional endpoint failures; across both subsets, audited successes changed from 853 to 860/900 for Cartesian–IK and from 850 to 858/900 for projection, while unrestricted OMPL remained 900/900. Among matched successes, changes in measured minimum obstacle clearance were small and their intervals included zero.

The replay remained consistent with shorter projected joint paths, but did not establish superior success rate or failure-penalized planning time for either GMM sampler. Because environments were reused, this analysis is a sensitivity study rather than new independent confirmation. Goal heights were unchanged; the effect of the difference between the saved training goal-height range and the evaluation range was not isolated.
