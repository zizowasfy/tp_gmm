# Figure captions

1. **Audited success and runtime of the three samplers** (`sampler_outcomes.pdf`). Points show equal-environment means across 120 environments, with pointwise 95% bootstrap intervals stratified by cohort, obstacle layout and requested clearance. Success labels are raw request counts; means retain equal environment weight despite different repeat counts. The success axis is expanded near 100%, and all-success intervals do not establish perfect reliability. PAR2 assigns a 6 s penalty to failures and caps successful action time at 3 s. Attributed pipeline latency includes shared GMM preparation for custom methods, request preparation/preflight and action latency, excluding common endpoint IK and offline path auditing.

2. **Distribution of path-quality summaries across environments** (`path_quality.pdf`). Each box summarizes the 120 environment-level means over successful requests for one method. Panels show weighted joint travel, EE path length and minimum padded robot–world clearance. Whiskers extend to 1.5 times the interquartile range and points beyond the whiskers are retained. These are descriptive successful-path summaries; paired effects use the common-success sets instead.

3. **Paired effects of sampler configuration on path quality** (`paired_sampler_effects.pdf`). Points are first-minus-second method effects averaged within environment over matched successful repeat pairs, then equally across environments. Error bars are pointwise 95% environment-cluster intervals. Negative path-length differences favor the first method; positive clearance differences favor the first method. The method pairings contain 896, 899 and 897 successful request pairs, respectively, across 120 environments each. Intervals are not simultaneous; inferential decisions use the reported Holm-adjusted tests.

4. **Setup cost, online sampling cost and proposal acceptance of the GMM methods** (`sampler_costs.pdf`). Left: descriptive per-request median times, in milliseconds. Setup includes anchor IK; online sampling includes its IK/FK and validity work. Medians are shown separately and do not sum to a median total. Right: mean per-environment ratio of valid sampler returns to attempted proposals, with 95% cluster intervals. These ratios describe sampler telemetry, not RRT-tree insertion rates. The baseline has no corresponding plugin counters and is omitted from this figure.

5. **Shared model-preparation stage costs** (`preparation_stages.pdf`). Bars show mean core times across 120 environments for model loading, TP-GMM reproduction, RL deformation and deformed GMR. Both GMM methods share the same per-environment model/reference preparation. These core stages exclude transport and other service overhead; pipeline latency uses the measured deformation-service wall time. Unrestricted OMPL requires no GMM preparation.

6. **Illustrative end-effector paths under the three sampler configurations** (`representative_paths.pdf`). The fixed example is environment 0, repeat 0, from the first cohort. It is not selected by outcome. Blue ellipsoids depict the spatial GMM proposal support at cutoff 3.0, the grey cylinder is the obstacle, and the colored curves are returned EE paths. All panels share spatial limits. The actual hard GMM corridor comprises enclosing boxes, while unrestricted OMPL has no GMM corridor. Robot links and the table mesh are omitted from this illustration but retained in the collision scene and path audit; the plot alone is not a collision certificate.
